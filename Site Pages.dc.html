About me

Hi, I'm Nicholas Bramhall — a Computer Engineering student at Georgia Institute of Technology with a goal to leave my mark on the hardware that defines modern computing.

Regardless of whether its GPUs and its role in AI or CPUs making everyday devices possible, I want to be able to build and design them knowing that when I hear someone talking about the new RTX, Ryzen core, or iPhone I played a role in making that device possible, and that its through these devices humanity is able to push the boundaries of computing.

I grew up in Wilmington, North Carolina, before moving to McDonough, Georgia after sophomore year of high school. Even at 12 years old, I knew that this is what I wanted when I helped my brother build his first PC and just the infatuation I was filled with in doing so! Seeing how all of these lifeless components are able to fit together so neatly and perfectly yet so intricately and making so many things possible. It's said as a joke but it really is tricking rocks into thinking! That passion eventually led me to Georgia Tech, where I'm now pursuing this field at one of the top engineering programs in the country.

Outside the classroom, I'm actively involved in the Baptist Collegiate Ministry and the Navigators, two communities that keep me grounded and connected on a large campus. I'm also looking to get involved with Silicon Jackets, GT's chip design club, where I hope to start building hands-on experience in the field I want to spend my career in.

Additionally, I spent much of my youth in Scouting earning the rank of Eagle. Through this, I developed many skills such as servant leadership, a service focused mindset, and the dedication to not give up what I've started, no matter how long it takes. Values I carry into college and still live by today, living "physically strong, mentally awake and morally straight."

Beyond organizations, my work experience spans more than just engineering — I've worked at Whataburger, served as a counselor at a Boy Scout summer camp, and currently work at the GT Career Center. These roles taught me how to show up, work hard, and communicate with people, fostering those interpersonal skills and the aspects of what makes humans humans and not someone replaceable by AI. Regardless of the exact future role or company I end up with after graduation, my impact will be immediate, meaningful, and lasting — and this is just the beginning.

Career goals

Digital design, for GPUs or CPUs

Where I'm headed, how the course load is built around it, and what still has to happen between now and graduation.

1.0 — The end goal

My ultimate end goal, as articulated elsewhere on this portfolio, is work as digital designer for either GPUs or CPUs. Thus as you will see below my course load is heavily ASIC and VLSI focused, but that doesn't mean I'm closed off to internships revolving around other areas such as analog or DSP and devices! As work of all kind can give holistic view of the hardware sector so that in the future I can better communicate with other teams!

2.0 — How?

With this in mind, how will this end goal be accomplished? As mentioned above my course load is heavily curated to that sector of computer engineering. Additionally my goal is that by the end of undergraduate I will have completed an internship and a co-op, thus getting experience in industry. After that I plan to do a BS/MS in order to dive deeper into the computer architecture of GPUs or CPUs and get it done in only 1 more year of schooling. Of course, right after that jump straight into industry, hopefully close to where I want to be but if not, just getting my foot in the door and working my way over to the CPU and GPU sector.
